INDIVIDUAL CASE SAFETY REPORT TASK 5
 ADR identification
A 62-year-old woman (68 kg) with a history of chronic kidney disease (Stage 3a) and hypertension was started on allopurinol 300 mg once daily for symptomatic gout. She also takes lisinopril 10 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg daily. On day 21 of allopurinol therapy, she presented with fever (38.9°C), a widespread morbilliform rash, facial edema, and cervical lymphadenopathy. Laboratory tests showed marked eosinophilia (1.8 × 10⁹/L) and acute kidney injury (creatinine rose to 2.1 mg/dL from a baseline of 1.1 mg/dL). Allopurinol was stopped on day 22. Skin biopsy confirmed Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) syndrome. She was hospitalized and started on IV systemic corticosteroids. Her rash and renal function are slowly improving, but she has not yet fully recovered.

The four minimum criteria are met: an identifiable patient, an identifiable reporter, a suspected drug, and an adverse event. Because the case is serious (requires inpatient hospitalization and carries life-threatening potential), it should be reported to the national pharmacovigilance centre within 15 calendar days.
3. Causality (WHO-UMC)
Certain. The temporal relationship aligns precisely with allopurinol-induced DRESS syndrome (typical 2–6 week onset window). The constellation of symptoms—fever, exanthem, eosinophilia, and renal impairment—is a characteristic severe adverse reaction pattern of allopurinol, particularly in patients with baseline renal impairment. Dechallenge was positive, with steady improvement upon drug discontinuation and corticosteroid therapy. Concomitant drugs (lisinopril, atorvastatin) have been taken long-term without issue and do not offer a plausible alternative explanation. Rechallenge was not performed and would be unsafe.
4. Classification
 * Seriousness: Serious. DRESS syndrome caused inpatient hospitalization, involves internal organ impairment (acute kidney injury), and is potentially life-threatening.
 * Expectedness: Expected. Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR), including DRESS syndrome, are highlighted in the allopurinol product warnings.
 * Severity: Severe. It requires emergency admission, systemic corticosteroid intervention, and intensive medical management.
